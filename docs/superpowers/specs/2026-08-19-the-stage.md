# SESH — The Stage

**Date:** 2026-08-19
**Status:** Proposed. Needs approval before a plan is written.
**Target:** v1 goal unset.
**Companion to:** `2026-08-19-identity-and-presence.md`, which this assumes.

---

## The defect, in one line of code

```
tv.ts:125   const pad = navigator.getGamepads?.()?.find((p) => p !== null);
```

SESH takes the **first** connected gamepad and discards every other one.

The vision's whole premise is a room that ties together the people in it. Its
deepest interaction flaw is that one person holds the controller and everybody
else watches — and that flaw is not a cultural fact about living rooms. It is a
`.find()`. A house that owns four DualShocks is already holding four independent
inputs that this product throws away.

Everything below follows from taking them.

## The reframe

Today the TV is a **launcher** and phones are **remote controls**. The Stage is
the claim that the TV is a **stage** and phones and controllers are **seats** —
the same hardware, a different product.

The audience is specific, and naming it correctly decides everything else:

> **The Stage serves whoever's hands are free.**

Six people, two controllers, a Smash set running: two people cannot look at
anything, and **four people have nothing in their hands.** Those four are the
Stage's users. It is not competing with the game for attention. It is serving
the people the game left out, which is most of the room for most of the night.

This is also what "interact frequently, or not" resolves to. Intensity is not a
preference someone sets; it tracks whether they are currently holding a
controller, and it changes several times an evening.

## The vocabulary

Five kinds of moment, separated because they obey different rules.

| | Direction | Example | Stakes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Announcement** | room → everyone | `sam's back — first time since march` | none, no input |
| **Ask** | room ⇄ everyone | *"what are we doing?"* → four tiles | a decision |
| **Claim** | person → room | *I've got next* | exclusive, ordered |
| **Reaction** | person → room | emoji popping on the TV | zero |
| **Report** | person → room | *I won* | factual, enters the record |

**Asks** are the Jackbox-shaped part, and the obvious application is choosing an
app by vote. One caution belongs in the spec rather than in a retrospective: an
ask beats one person on the D-pad **only when nobody has already decided.** When
someone knows they want Kodi, a poll is friction wearing a party hat.

**Reactions** are the one this spec is least confident in, and says so. A
reaction needs something to react to, and during a match the people who care
most are holding controllers. Reactions are likely a **party-mode** feature —
a room where nobody is playing and the TV is a shared object people are near —
rather than a hangout feature. Build them second.

## First feature: the turn queue

In a room with more people than controllers, **the most contested social
resource is who plays next.** It is currently managed by shouting, by whoever is
loudest, and by the person who has been waiting forty minutes and is too polite
to mention it. Every house has this. Nothing solves it.

- Tap **next** on a phone, or press a button on a spare pad. Your mascot joins
  the line on the TV.
- Winner stays on — the house rule the queue already assumes.
- Advancement is **manual**, exactly like score reporting. No game integration,
  now or ever.
- Events: `turn.claimed` (actor), `turn.started` (both players as actors),
  `turn.ended`.

### The queue is advisory, never authoritative

If somebody hands the controller to a friend out of order, **the room must not
argue.** It updates, or it is corrected. Enforcement in a living room is
obnoxious and would be the fastest way to make people stop using this.

This is the same posture as `asserted` presence in the companion spec, and it
has now appeared often enough to be a law rather than a habit:

> **The room shows what it believes, and can always be told it is wrong.**

### The controller hog is the aux-cord problem in a different hat

The shared queue exists so that nobody is hostage to one person's playlist. The
turn queue, using the **same majority mechanic**, means nobody is hostage to one
person's win streak: a majority of the people present can agree that
forty-five minutes is long enough.

Identical projection, identical veto, identical social function. This is the
third payout of the vision's own core insight — build the log once and features
stop being separate projects.

**Decided: the majority bump ships with the turn queue**, not after it. It is
the feature's soul, and a turn queue without it is a list rather than a
mechanic.

## The overlay

Whether the Stage may interrupt a running app. Measured on TatePi rather than
predicted, per this project's rule about off-hardware claims.

**Verified on the hardware:**

- **`labwc 0.8.4` advertises `zwlr_layer_shell_v1` version 4.** Read live off
  the running compositor's registry on 2026-08-20, on the same socket the kiosk
  is already connected to — not inferred from the fact that labwc is
  wlroots-based. The protocol built precisely for surfaces that draw above
  fullscreen clients is there, at the version the overlay would use.
- **The kiosk is Chromium, and Chromium does not speak layer-shell.** Neither
  `/usr/bin/chromium` nor `/usr/bin/chromium-browser` contains the
  `zwlr_layer_shell` symbol anywhere in the binary. The overlay cannot be the
  existing web surface. It has to be a separate, small Wayland client.

**Not verified, and load-bearing:**

- **Moonlight is the hard case.** It is a latency-sensitive fullscreen stream,
  and compositing over it on a Pi 5 costs something that has not been measured.

> **Both of the above moved from prediction to measurement on 2026-08-20**, and
> neither moved the design: layer-shell is present, Chromium still cannot reach
> it, and the conclusion below is the one the spec already drew. Recorded
> because "wlroots-based and therefore" is an inference, and this project keeps
> getting bitten by inferences written down as facts.

The constraint turns out to be a gift:

> **Because the overlay must be a separate surface, it can only ever be small.**
> It cannot grow into a rich screen. The technical limit and the rarity law from
> the companion spec arrive at the same answer independently.

**Position: a thin strip, reserved for what passes the rarity bar.** `sam's back
— first time since march` earns an interruption. `music.started` does not. The
Stage stays *a place the TV goes*; the overlay is a whisper from that place, not
the place itself.

## The controller

### Multi-pad is not an enhancement, it is the point

> **Single-controller input reintroduces the exact flaw SESH exists to fix.
> Multi-controller input is what makes couch input consistent with the thesis.**

Same hardware, opposite meaning. Iterating the Gamepad API instead of `find`-ing
it turns one voice into four, with **no phone, no scanning, and no new
hardware** — which reaches precisely the people who never get their phone out,
and who are otherwise invisible to everything in the companion spec.

### The light bar

**Verified on this Pi:** `hid-sony.ko.xz` and `hid-playstation.ko.xz` both ship
with kernel `6.12.96+rpt-rpi-2712`. `/sys/class/leds/` exists and currently holds
only board LEDs (`ACT`, `PWR`, `mmc*`) because the controller is powered off.
`hid-nintendo` and `hid-steam` are present too, so Switch Pro and Steam pads are
the same story.

The companion spec decided that **the room assigns your mascot** rather than
letting you pick it. So the room can assign your colour as well — and then:

> **The controller in your hand glows the colour the room decided you are.**

That is identity leaving the screen and entering somebody's hands. It is the
most physical expression of *"a living room that knows who's in it"* that this
hardware can produce, and the driver for it is already on the disk.

**Not verified:** that the light bar is actually settable through the LED class
on this kernel with this pad, which needs the controller switched on.

### Claiming a pad

Pick up the second controller and the room **guesses** it is you, because you
claimed next thirty seconds ago — and you correct it if it is wrong. Same
posture as everything else; see the law above.

### Bluetooth contention, and why it is not a blocker

The Pi already streams A2DP to the Victrola, and stacking several Bluetooth
controllers onto the same radio is a known source of audio stutter and input lag
on Pis — which would directly threaten the thing Arc 2 just got working.

**Resolved: the controller is wired.** Tate has the cable. Bluetooth remains
available, but nothing in this spec depends on it. A second USB Bluetooth
dongle is the fallback if wireless pads are ever wanted at the same time as
music.

> **Correction, 2026-08-20: the bond does not stay, because there is no bond.**
> This paragraph originally read "the bond stays (`A4:AE:12:9C:D1:3C  Wireless
> Controller` is in BlueZ today)". BlueZ knows the pad's *name*; it does not
> hold a *key*. `/var/lib/bluetooth/<adapter>/A4:AE:12:9C:D1:3C/info` contains
> `[General]` and `[DeviceID]` and no key section of any kind, and
> `bluetoothctl devices Paired` lists only the Basilisk mouse — which has
> `[IdentityResolvingKey]` and `[LongTermKey]`, and is the one device here that
> genuinely reconnects.
>
> That is the identical shape #30 diagnosed on the Victrola, landed three hours
> before this spec was written, and it got repeated here by reading "appears in
> `bluetoothctl devices`" as "bonded". `Trusted=true` without a key is the same
> red herring in both cases.
>
> **Nothing in the spec moves.** The controller is wired and the sentence was
> load-bearing for nothing — which is exactly why it is worth correcting now,
> while it is still cheap and before a plan quotes it.

## Hangout and party are different products

The companion spec establishes both tiering and party mode. The Stage is where
the split becomes sharpest.

| | Hangout (5–8) | Party (20–40) |
|---|---|---|
| Hands free | some, rotating | everyone |
| Music | shared queue, majority veto | request line, no voting — sixteen votes will never happen |
| Turn queue | **the main event** | irrelevant, nobody is playing |
| Reactions | thin | **the main event** |
| Asks | works well | needs a much lower bar to answer |
| The TV | a participant | wallpaper with occasional spikes |

These are not two configurations of one screen. They are close to two products
sharing a log, and pretending otherwise produces something mediocre at both.

## The laws

1. **Nothing may ever require everyone.** Anything that blocks on full
   participation dies the first time someone is in the bathroom.
2. **The Stage never removes the fast path.** The grid and the controller stay
   exactly as they are; every ask is reachable, never mandatory.
3. **The Stage serves whoever's hands are free**, and must be nearly invisible
   to whoever's are not.
4. **Silence is a valid answer.** Every ask needs a graceful timeout, because
   most will get two responses out of six.
5. **The room may be funny about you, never unkind about you.** A room that
   assigns identity has to be generous with it — unlike a picker, nobody can opt
   out of what it decides.
6. **The room shows what it believes, and can always be told it is wrong.**

## Behaviour to settle in the plan

- What advances the turn queue, and who is allowed to advance it.
- Whether the majority bump uses the same denominator as the music veto, and
  therefore inherits every presence question in the companion spec.
- Whether a claim can be made from a controller or only from a phone.
- What the Stage shows when the room is idle — this is where it meets the
  roster, and the two must be one screen rather than two.
- Whether an ask can be answered by a pad that has not been claimed by anyone.

## Testing

- **The turn queue is a projection over events** and is unit-testable exactly as
  the music queue is: seed claims and results, assert the line. This is where
  the logic lives.
- **Multi-pad enumeration must be checked in the kiosk Chromium**, not assumed.
  The Gamepad API's behaviour with several pads under Wayland is precisely the
  class of interaction — correct component, wrong behaviour across a boundary —
  that cost Arc 2 four bugs and that a green suite cannot see.
- **The light bar, the overlay over Moonlight, and pad enumeration are all
  hardware-bound** and are verified in the room.
- The turn queue's real test is a night with more people than controllers, and
  the question is whether anybody stopped shouting.

## Not in scope

- **Reactions**, beyond naming them and arguing they are party-mode.
- **Automatic match detection.** Manual reporting is the design, per the
  vision's deferred ingest decision.
- **Party mode's own surfaces.** The table above scopes it; it needs its own
  spec.
- **Replacing the grid.** Law 2.

## Open questions for approval

**1. Does the Stage and the roster share one screen?** The companion spec argues
the roster is Attract Mode's honest first draft. The Stage wants the same
pixels when idle. They are probably one design, and treating them as two will
produce two half-screens.

**2. Can an unclaimed pad vote?** A controller nobody has claimed is an
anonymous input. Letting it answer keeps friction at zero; refusing means every
vote has an actor and enters the record honestly. The log has a strong opinion
about anonymity elsewhere in this project.
